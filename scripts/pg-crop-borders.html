Picture Guide — strip borders, standardise sizes

Photos taken out of the printed guide carry a border drawn into the image itself: a 3px line with a 1px antialias fringe outside it, and on the red ones a second fringe inside too, so black borders are 4px and red ones are 5px. The app draws its own frame and its own OPTIONAL flag, so that border lands as a second outline just inside the first. This finds the border on each photo, trims it off, and puts the trimmed photo back under the same filename — nothing in the database changes. It then puts every photo onto one standard canvas — 593×593, the same for every sheet in the guide — so all the boxes on the board are identical and each photo fills its own completely.

It pads; it does not crop to fit. That is measured, not assumed. The exports arrive in two shapes, 591×565 for iPhones and 563×593 for Android, and trimming either one down to a shared shape would cut about 14px off two sides. Every one of the ten Android photos has its subject within 12px of the top or bottom edge, and five of the fourteen iPhone photos have theirs touching the left or right — the phone body, the charging port, the notch. So the short side is filled out instead, with the photo's own backdrop colour sampled from its edge, which is why the padding does not read as a border. A photo larger than the canvas in some direction is centre-cropped down to it, but only by the few pixels the export happened to be over.

Edit NAMES below for a new batch. The canvas is GUIDE, and .pg-frame in styles.css is square to match — those two go together, and a batch left un-standardised shows up on the board as a letterboxed card rather than quietly reshaping the whole sheet.

Scan first and read the border column. A batch whose borders were already removed before saving should report none on every row — that is the expected result, not a failure, and it means the crop is a pure centring. Any row claiming a border on a photo you know is clean is a reason to stop rather than to continue: this trims pixels off originals and puts them back under the same name.
